Narrative Past Tense — Telling the Story of a Change

01 — NOTICE THE PATTERN

Read. Don't explain yet — just notice.

Click any underlined phrase in the sentences below. Look for what's different between them before you scroll on.

Everyday I used to walk to school every morning when I was a kid.
Professional We used to review onboarding feedback only once a year.
Everyday Last Tuesday I walked to the pharmacy because my car wouldn't start.
Professional Last quarter we reviewed the onboarding feedback with the whole team.
Everyday While I was walking home, it started to rain.
Professional While we were reviewing the data, we noticed a gap in one region.

Marker note: pairs tagged 1 describe something repeated/habitual, 2 describe one finished action, 3 describe something in progress when something else happened. You don't need the labels yet — just notice the shape of each sentence.

02 — CONCEPT CHECK

Test what you noticed

No rule has been given yet on purpose. Answer based on the pattern you just saw.

1. Which sentence describes something that happened repeatedly, over a long period, and no longer happens?

"Last quarter we reviewed the onboarding feedback."
"We used to review onboarding feedback once a year."
"While we were reviewing the data, we noticed a gap."

2. Which form is used for a single completed action at a specific past time?

used to + verb
simple past
was/were + -ing

3. "While we ___ the data, we noticed a gap" — which form fits, and why?

reviewed — because it's a finished action
were reviewing — because it was in progress when something else happened
used to review — because it happened repeatedly

4. Which sentence implies the practice is no longer done today?

"We used to approve access requests manually."
"We approved the access request yesterday."
"We were approving access requests when the system went down."

5. Which sentence describes two things happening at once, where one gives background to the other?

"We used to hold town halls every quarter."
"We held a town hall last Friday."
"We were holding a town hall when the CEO announced the merger."

6. Which sentence would you use to describe one specific action with a stated time, like "in March"?

"We used to run engagement surveys twice a year."
"We ran an engagement survey in March."
"We were running the engagement survey when response rates spiked."
03 — THE RULE

Confirming what you found

Three ways to talk about the past • used to + verb — a repeated habit or state that no longer continues ("we used to review it yearly").
• simple past — a single completed action at a specific time ("we reviewed it last quarter").
• was/were + -ing — an action in progress when something else happened ("we were reviewing it when we noticed the gap").

The mistake to watch for: reaching for simple past by default for everything, even habitual or backgrounded actions — that's the pattern to unlearn.

05 — APPLICATION

Use it for real

This session's application is a writing task. Listening/output practice will come from the video segment in the live class.

Think of one HR program, process, or initiative you've been closely involved with — an onboarding process, a DEI initiative, an engagement survey, a talent review cycle, anything from your own experience.

Write 5–8 sentences describing how it changed over time. Use all three forms at least once: used to, simple past, and past continuous.
