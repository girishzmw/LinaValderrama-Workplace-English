Session 4 · Pre-Learning

If things had gone differently…

Conditionals for scenario planning — imagining the present, and reckoning with the past. About 40 minutes, five short stages.

01Notice the pattern

Two kinds of "what if"

Read the eight sentences below. Don't worry about naming the grammar yet — just notice what each one is really talking about.

Still possible / imagined
Everyday

If I had a bigger kitchen, I would host dinner parties every month.

Work

If our engagement score dropped 15% next quarter, we would launch an emergency listening tour.

Everyday

If she lived closer to the office, she wouldn't need a car.

Work

If the budget doubled, HR would hire three more business partners.

Already over / can't be changed
Everyday

If I had left the house five minutes earlier, I wouldn't have missed the bus.

Work

If the team had rolled out the pulse survey in January, they would have caught the warning signs sooner.

Everyday

If she had known about the traffic, she would have taken the metro instead.

Work

If HR had communicated the restructuring plan earlier, employees wouldn't have felt blindsided.

02Concept check

What are these sentences actually doing?

Answer based on what you noticed above — there's no rule box yet, just your own observation.

1. The four "Still possible / imagined" sentences are about a time that is —

2. The four "Already over" sentences describe events that —

3. Look at "If I had a bigger kitchen" and "If she lived closer." What verb form follows if in the "still possible" group?

4. Now look at "If I had left" and "If she had known." What verb form follows if in the "already over" group?

5. In "If our engagement score dropped 15%..." — is that drop something that has actually happened?

6. Compare "If our engagement score dropped..." (still possible) with "If the team had rolled out the survey in January..." (already over). What's the key difference between them?

Answer all six questions to unlock the rule.

03The rule

Confirming what you found

This is exactly what you noticed above — just named properly now.

2nd Conditional — unreal present / future

If + past simple, ... would / could / might + base verb

For a situation that is imagined, unlikely, or hypothetical — but still possible in principle, now or later.
If the score dropped 15%, we would launch a listening tour.

3rd Conditional — unreal past

If + past perfect, ... would / could / might + have + past participle

For a situation in the past that did not happen — and cannot be changed now. Pure "what if it had been different."
If the team had rolled out the survey in January, they would have caught the signs sooner.

One important habit: keep each sentence entirely in one type or the other. Don't mix a past-simple "if" clause with a "would have" result — that blends the two forms. If the past perfect part of this feels shaky after last session, that's exactly the connection worth noticing: the 3rd conditional is really just past perfect wearing a hypothetical hat.

04Consolidation quiz

Complete each sentence

Type the correct form of the verb in brackets. Check your answers when you're done with all six.

1. If we (survey) employees more often, we (notice) the drop earlier.

2. If I (have) more vacation days, I (travel) more.

3. If the team (communicate) the change sooner, staff (not / feel) blindsided.

4. If our turnover rate (increase) again this year, what (you / do)?

5. If she (know) about the reorganization, she (not / resign).

6. If I (be) the HR director, I (change) the onboarding process.

05Application — write it out

Your own scenario

This one's handwritten, not typed.

Write 5–8 sentences

Describe a risk or scenario-planning situation from your own career. Include:

  • At least one 2nd conditional sentence — what you would do if a similar situation happened again.
  • At least one 3rd conditional sentence — looking back, what you would have done differently.
