3 · The rule (what you just found)

You have already worked this out. This is a summary to confirm it and to keep for reference.

1. Move the verb one step back (“backshift”)

When the report happens later, the verb moves one step into the past.

They saidYou report
am / is / arewas / were
present continuous (are reviewing)past continuous (were reviewing)
present perfect (have worked)past perfect (had worked)
past simple (lost)past perfect (had lost). In conversation, the past simple is often kept when the order of events is clear.
will / can / may / mustwould / could / might / had to
past perfect, would, could, might, shouldno change (there is no further step back)

2. Reported questions become statements

  • Use statement word order: subject + verb. No do / did, no question mark.
  • Yes/no questions → if / whether: The survey asked employees whether they felt valued.
  • Wh- questions keep the question word: She asked me where I had bought my jacket.

3. Requests and instructions

ask / tell + person + (not) to + verb: My manager asked me to send her the report. Legal told us not to share the results.

4. Say vs. tell

tell + person: He told me (that)… · say (that) or say to + person: He said (that)… / He said to me…

5. Time and place words follow the new moment

OriginalReported later / elsewhere
today · tomorrow · yesterdaythat day · the next / following day · the day before
last quarter · next monththe previous quarter · the following month
here · this · nowthere · that · then

If you report on the same day and in the same place, keep the original words.

6. When backshift is optional

If the information is still true now, you can keep the present tense: She told me their head office is in Bogotá. Backshifting is also correct. Keeping the present shows you believe it is still true.

7. Reporting verbs: each one has its own pattern

Professional reports rarely use only say and tell. Learn each verb together with its pattern:

PatternVerbsExample
verb + that…admit, explain, claim, acknowledge, confirm, point out, argueThe VP acknowledged that turnover had been underestimated.
verb + person + to…ask, advise, urge, encourage, remind, warnMy coach advised me to talk to my team first.
verb + -ingsuggest, recommend, admit, denyAna suggested running a pulse survey.
verb + to…offer, agree, refuse, promiseMy brother offered to pick the kids up.
Watch out: Spanish patterns that don’t work in English
  • ✗ He explained me the policy → ✓ He explained the policy to me
  • ✗ She suggested me to apply → ✓ She suggested applying / suggested that I apply
  • ✗ He said me that… → ✓ He told me that… / He said that…
  • ✗ She asked if did I agree → ✓ She asked if I agreed

Report it to a colleague who wasn’t there. Explain who said what, what was asked, and what was recommended.

You can change names and details. What matters is the language.
