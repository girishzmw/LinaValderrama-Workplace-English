Have, has, had — one family of tenses

Present perfect, present perfect continuous, and past perfect: three tenses, one shared mechanism. About 40 minutes, five short stages.

01Notice the pattern

Three tenses, one family

Read the ten sentences below. Notice what they have in common — and notice where they differ.

Present Perfect — no specific time, still relevant now
Everyday

I have visited Cartagena three times.

Work

She has led five transformation projects at three different companies.

Present Perfect Continuous — emphasizes duration, still going
Everyday

I have been learning Portuguese for six months.

Work

The team has been rolling out the new onboarding platform since March.

Past Perfect — earlier than another point in the past
Everyday

By the time I arrived, the meeting had already started.

Work

Before she joined GeoPark, the company had already restructured twice.

One more contrast — specified vs. unspecified time
Everyday

I ate lunch at noon.
vs.
I have already eaten lunch.

Work

We implemented the policy in March.
vs.
We have implemented three new policies this year.

03The rule

One mechanism, three uses

This is exactly what you noticed above — just named properly now.

Present Perfect

have / has + past participle

A finished action or experience with no specific time stated, connected to now. Often used for achievements, experience, or an unfinished period (this year, so far).
She has led five transformation projects.

Present Perfect Continuous

have / has been + verb-ing

Same "now" anchor, but the focus shifts to duration — how long something has been going on, often still ongoing. Common with "for" and "since."
The team has been rolling out the platform since March.

Past Perfect

had + past participle

The anchor point moves into the past — describes what had already happened before that past moment.
Before she joined, the company had already restructured twice.

The whole family = auxiliary (have / has / had) + past participle. The auxiliary's tense tells you where your "now" is — actual now, or a moment back in the past.
